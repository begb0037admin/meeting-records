## Slide 2

For H&S – In June we handled 38 tasks in total – up four on May. Cority rose from 14 to 17, IRIS climbed to 16 – up from 14 in May and Odyssey 6 to 5. This month-to-month shuffling between the three systems is a natural variation, OH tends to be reactive and unpredictable rather than following a consistent pattern. As you know we're still building the dataset for H&S, so the trend will become clearer as the year goes on.

## Slide 3

Looking at how quickly tasks were resolved – 32 tasks were completed in June, down from 39 in May. Same-day resolution is now the largest band at 13 tasks (40.6%), overtaking the 6+ day band, which has shrunk to12 tasks (37.5%) – down from over half (51.3%) in May. Next-day resolution held steady at 3 tasks, and 3–5 days fell to 4 tasks. Its worth noting the 6+ day figure are mostly tasks waiting on the customer for information, or on a third-party to respond. But this month’s shift toward faster same-day resolution is a positive signal.

## Slide 4

June came in at 313 PXD tasks across all categories, down from 416 in May. Year-on-year, that's 313 against 364 in June 2025, a 14 percent decrease. Service requests accounted for most of the movement, down from 296 to 150. HR Self Service moved the other way, up from 20 to 47. Incident – Other rose slightly to 101 and Change to 15. Volumes across these categories continue to move month to month. We’ll keep tracking the pattern over the coming months.

## Slide 5

Looking at where the work came from. On service requests, Manager Self Service was the single biggest category at nearly half the total volume, 46 percent at 69 tasks. Work Group came in at 35 and HR Systems User Access at 31. Together those three account for 90 percent of all service request work. With the WFM project now complete and all departments live, these volumes represent a settled picture of BAU support. So what we’re seeing here - is the ongoing operational footprint of a fully deployed system – and a useful baseline, because we now have a clearer sense of what support looks like for WFM. On the incident side, People Management led at 28 tasks with Time and Attendance at 18 and Data Protection Request at 7. Together those three categories account for 78 percent of all reactive work, and with WFM fully live, this is the level of ongoing demand we should expect to carry each month."

## Slide 6

Service requests met both KPI thresholds in June. Same or next day came in at 82 percent – up on May’s 80 and comfortably above the 75 percent green threshold. Less than five days was 98 percent, also up on May’s 95. 112 of the 150 service requests were completed on the day they came in, which is a strong result. Maintaining those numbers with a small team is something worth acknowledging.

## Slide 7

Incident completion times remain a mixed picture this month. Same or next day came in at 74.0 percent – now inside the 65–75 percent amber band, an improvement on May’s 63.9 percent, which was red. Less than five days is 87.0 percent against a 90 percent amber threshold, still red, but up on May’s 82. Incident – other tasks can be complex – cases where it takes longer to pin down the root cause. With the team also handling 150 service requests this month, the time available for that investigative work gets really squeezed. Over 10 tasks ran to six or more days. It’s seems like a small number, but on a small team with no slack, it's about capacity, not performance.

## Slide 8

Task acceptance time was 0.4 days in June, down from 0.5 in May, a big improvement on this time last year. Tasks are typically being picked up within the same day or the following morning, well inside the 1.5-day green threshold. This metric has been consistently green throughout 2026. It’s a clean result, and a positive reflection of the team.

## Slide 9

Average completion time dropped again in June – 1.4 days, down from 1.7 in May, and well below the 3.5-day green threshold. A year ago, in June 2025 the average was 2.4 days, so the improvement over twelve months remains solid. To put the trajectory in context: the peak we recorded was 8.3 days in February 2025, then 6.5 in October, 4.1 in January 2026, 1.7 in May, and we’re now at 1.4 – that’s the lowest monthly figure we’ve recorded. If the balance of work shifts, more Change tasks, more complex incident work returning at scale, the average will move up again. But the direction over the past year is genuinely strong, and 1.4 days is a new low.

## Slide 10

In June the team completed 322 tasks against 347 created – a negative variance of 25, breaking a run of five consecutive months of positive variance. Year on year, we created fewer tasks in June 2026 than June 2025 and completed 55 fewer – so both volume and completions are down against last year, a reversal from the growth of recent months. To reiterate, our team operating at this level still has no headroom, there’s no capacity to absorb a sustained spike in demand. This month’s negative variance is a reminder that the margin the team operates on can move in either direction.
